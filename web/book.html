✓ Downloading Worldline.dmg — then:

  1. Open Worldline.dmg and drag Worldline onto Applications.
  2. Open Worldline. The first time, macOS says it can't check the app (it isn't from the App Store) — click Done. Worldline is free and open source, so it isn't registered with Apple's paid program.

Quick add

Type it, and it's on your calendar.

One box for events, tasks and deadlines, in plain English — with a preview of exactly what you'll get before you press Return.

  • lunch with Sam tomorrow 12:30 #social for 90 min
  • Change things the same way: move Alex's meeting to tues at 3pm, skip gym this week
  • Deadlines get a runway that changes colour as the day gets close
  • Reminders before events and deadlines, as Mac notifications

Calendars

Every calendar you have, both ways.

The calendars already on your Mac — iCloud, Google, Exchange — show up and can be edited here; changes go back to them.

  • Subscribe to any iCal link: seminars, a team's schedule, a Google calendar
  • Repeating events, time zones and travel handled properly
  • Export any calendar as an .ics file, back everything up in one file

Habits

Habits that hold up over years.

Strength instead of a fragile streak: missing one day doesn't wipe out months. Every year of history stays.

  • Daily, a few times a week, or an amount (pages, minutes)
  • Rest days that don't count against you
  • Ticks itself from your calendar (“counts gym events”)

Projects

Projects you write in.

Long-running work as pages and dated notes. Worldline gathers what's in them: your to-dos, the ideas you jotted, who you're waiting on and what's still open.

  • LaTeX equations, inline and displayed
  • Code blocks with syntax colours — Python, Bash, C++, Julia, Fortran, SQL…
  • Search across every page and note

Mac and iPhone

On your phone too — synced, encrypted.

Pair your iPhone with a QR code. Changes go between your devices end-to-end encrypted, through public relays that only ever see ciphertext.

  • Works offline on both; catches up when it's back
  • The iPhone app opens from Safari — no App Store, nothing to pay
  • No account: the pairing code is the only key
